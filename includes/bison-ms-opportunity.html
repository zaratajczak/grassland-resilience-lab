MS Student Opportunity: Bison Ecology, Behavior, and Reintroduction at Konza Prairie

Grassland Resilience Lab at Kansas State University and Konza Prairie Biological Station, led by Dr. Zak Ratajczak

We are looking for an MS student to work at the interface of bison ecology, animal behavior, and technology. The student would work on two primary projects, with opportunities to develop additional research questions based on their own interests.

1. Bison behavior and experimental drought. Our first project will use two years of camera trap data to understand how bison respond to the infrastructure used to experimentally impose drought. This is an existing dataset, allowing the student to begin analyzing data and addressing ecological questions early in their graduate career.

2. Bison movement and reintroduction. We recently outfitted 28 bison with CERES tags, which are designed to measure animal location and behavior. In 2027, we will use these tags to establish baseline movement patterns and behavior for individual bison. Then, in 2028, bison will be reintroduced to a portion of Konza Prairie where they have been absent for over 100 years.

This creates an exciting opportunity to follow the same individual bison before and after they gain access to a new landscape. How quickly will they explore this unfamiliar habitat? Will some individuals be more adventurous than others? How will their movement and behavior change as they encounter new terrain, vegetation, and resources?

Our goal is to understand how bison respond to new landscapes, providing insights that can inform reintroductions and conservation planning in other grasslands. We also hope to learn more about bison behavior and ecology, contributing to more fundamental questions in biology.

3. Student-driven research questions. In addition to these projects, we encourage students to develop research questions of their own. Konza Prairie has a wealth of long-term data, ongoing experiments, and opportunities for field research. Depending on the student’s interests, there are opportunities to explore questions related to bison behavior, grazing, fire, climate, vegetation, and other aspects of grassland ecology. We want students to have a meaningful role in shaping their research, while working within the broader goals of the funded projects.

Requirements

The minimum requirements are:

  • A background in one or more of the following areas: ecology, wildlife biology, animal behavior, GIS, quantitative biology, or related fields.
  • The ability and willingness to work outdoors, sometimes in hot weather and challenging conditions.
  • Basic computer literacy and a willingness to learn new analytical techniques.
  • Demonstrated ability to work independently, take initiative, and follow through on projects.
  • Good communication skills and an interest in developing as a scientific writer.
  • Intellectual curiosity and an enthusiasm for learning.
  • A valid driver’s license.

The ideal candidate would also have:

  • Demonstrated field experience. This can range from scientific research to jobs outside of science where you worked outdoors.
  • Experience working with geospatial data in ArcGIS, QGIS, or similar software.
  • Familiarity with coding, ideally in R, although experience with Python or other languages is also valuable.
  • Experience working in the Great Plains or similar ecosystems.

We don’t expect applicants to have all of these preferred qualifications. We’re particularly interested in finding a motivated, curious student who is excited about the science and willing to develop new skills.

Other advantages of this position

We are an inclusive lab and welcome students and collaborators from a wide range of backgrounds. Our collaborators bring a diversity of personal experiences and scientific perspectives. We are firm believers that a wider range of ideas and life experiences leads to better science and a greater impact on society.

Konza Prairie is a long-term, NSF-funded research site where scientists have spent decades quantifying the interactions between bison, cattle grazing, fire, climate, and grassland ecosystems. The student would have access to a wealth of additional data, including long-term measurements of bison weight and reproduction, a network of over 600 permanent vegetation plots, and high-resolution aerial imagery, including LiDAR and hyperspectral data. We also have established workflows for data cleaning, spatial analysis, time series analysis, and machine learning that can help bring these different sources of data together.

Students at Konza also have opportunities to connect with a broad network of scientists working across environmental science. We place a strong emphasis on developing research, analytical, writing, and communication skills that will be useful across a range of careers.

Living in Manhattan, Kansas

Kansas State University and the city of Manhattan offer a high quality of life, with a relatively low cost of living and plenty to do outside of work. Manhattan has two downtown areas, each with restaurants, breweries, and other attractions. There are also multiple hiking and biking trails within 15 minutes of town, featuring surprisingly hilly terrain. Two nearby lakes and the Kansas River offer opportunities for boating, swimming, and other outdoor recreation.

The nearby city of Lawrence has a larger downtown and multiple concert venues. Kansas City, about two hours away, offers the amenities of a larger city, and the Rocky Mountains are within a day’s drive. Manhattan also has a convenient regional airport, about 15 minutes from campus.

Funding will come from a combination of NSF research support during the summers and teaching assistantships during portions of the academic year.
